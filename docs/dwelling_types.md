# Structural type of dwelling


## [Comparability of concepts over time](https://www12.statcan.gc.ca/census-recensement/2021/ref/98-500/001/98-500-x2021001-eng.cfm)
The structural type of dwelling concept has remained relatively stable since the 1981 Census. Though there were no changes between the 2016 and 2011 censuses, there have been some adjustments to the concepts and classifications in earlier censuses. In 2011, additional information was added for classification within definitions for 'row house' and 'apartment in a building that has five or more storeys.' Starting in 2006, 'apartment or flat in a duplex' includes duplexes attached to other dwellings or buildings. Prior to the 2006 Census, duplexes attached to other dwellings or buildings were classified as an 'apartment in a building that has fewer than five storeys.' Starting in 1981, dwellings in apartment buildings are classified in two categories, 'apartment in a building that has five or more storeys' and 'apartment in a building that has fewer than five storeys.' Previously, this distinction between the heights of apartment buildings was not available and these types of dwellings were all classified as 'apartments.' Since there are more variants in the classification of structural type of dwelling prior to the 1981 Census, users are cautioned to use census reference materials when making historical comparisons that predate the 1981 Census.

Table 3 notes that: Other classifies 'other single-attached house' for census data. For LFS data, the response 'Other-specify' may contain additional types of dwellings not covered by the census classification.

Related notes include:
- Note 1: Single attached includes the following census structural type of dwelling classifications: "semi-detached," "row house," "other single-attached."
- Note 2: Apartment includes the following census structural type of dwelling classifications: "apartment or flat in a duplex," "apartment in a building with five or more storeys," "apartment in a building with fewer than five storeys."
- Note 3: Other includes the following census structural type of dwelling classifications: "mobile home," "other movable dwellings."
- Note 4: Percentage distributions are calculated on rounded data and may not necessarily add up to 100%.

# Reconciling the dataset
To reconcile the datset, the strategy is to: 
1 - first use the data by dwelling type when available, since this data is more accessible and generally better documented (based on 100%); 
2 - Sum the dwelling types in the four categories (single detached, single attached, apartment, mobile); add a marker or star or something so I know these are the target categories, and are possibly duplicated.
3 - As far as possible, interpolate data based on available dwelling types
4 - reconcile it by distributing additional dwellings on the period of construction by weight;

In fact, this seems to be a classic [Iterative proportional fitting problem](https://pypi.org/project/ipfn/), also described [here](https://datascience.oneoffcoder.com/ipf.html) and discussed [here](https://stats.stackexchange.com/questions/37771/iterative-proportional-fitting-and-independent-variables), followed by [specialized rounding](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/44737874/rounding-floats-while-maintaining-total-sum-equal) if I want the sums to be correct; normal rounding should work fine given the error range I work with here.

## Reconciling the dwelling types
One issue when reconciling the dwelling types is the change of definitions between 'other_dwelling' (from 1986 to 2011) and 'other_single_attached' (in 2006, 2011) to 'other_attached_dwellings' (after 2016), which is defined as:
> The category 'Other attached-dwelling' is a subtotal of the following categories: semi-detached house, row house, apartment or flat in a duplex, apartment in a building that has fewer than five storeys and other single-attached house

It contains both the 'single_attached' type, i.e. semi-detached, row, other single attached, and 'apartments', i.e. flats/duplexes, apartments<5, apartments>5. Though row houses are arguably not 'single' attached per-se, this fits the older definitions (see e.g. 1961 census in 1961935291963engfra.pdf)

This mismatch confused me at first, however after checking, they have the **same** definition. 'other_dwelling' is short for 'other_multiple_dwelling'. For 1986, 1991, 1996, 2006, 2011, the 'other_dwelling' category is equivalent to total - single_detached - apartment>5 - mobile, which is the exact same definition as 'other_attached_dwellings' in 2016 and 2021.

However, this definition does **not** fit the NEUD definition, where apartments occupy the major share of buildings after single_detached. Thus, I'll calculate the 'other_attached_dwelling' for consistency, but I'll also sum the types by single_attached and apartments (thus splitting the 'other_single_attached' definition)
